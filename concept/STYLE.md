# Tokenmaxxing 2: You're Absolutely Right — concept art bible

The sequel flips the camera. In Tokenmaxxing 1 you were the developer hiring
agents. In Tokenmaxxing 2 you ARE the agent, living inside the laptop, and the
developer is the one on the other side of the screen typing "ok do it".

## Style (every image)

- Match Tokenmaxxing 1 (reference screenshots): dark, cozy, chunky pixel art
  with visible square pixels and ordered dithering for light falloff. No
  painterly rendering, no 3D, no photorealism, no smooth gradients.
- Palette: near-black slate background (#14171c to #2a2f38); terminal green
  (#4ade5a) as the hero accent, with a soft dithered glow; amber (#f2b33d) for
  highlights and warnings; muted red (#e0524a) for danger and cost; occasional
  soft purple (#9b6bd6) and sky blue (#4aa3f0) for pickups. Mostly dark, with
  small bright accents.
- In-image text: blocky monospace pixel font, uppercase, wide letter spacing,
  like the TOKENMAXXING title in the reference. Render quoted text verbatim.
- Tone: deadpan developer humour. The joke lives in the objects and the text,
  not in exaggerated cartoon faces.

## Cast

**THE AGENT (the player).** A small upright creature shaped like a blinking
text cursor: a tall glowing block about twice as tall as it is wide, terminal
green with a lighter inner core and a soft dithered green glow. Two simple
square white-pixel eyes near the top. Thin stick arms and short stick legs, 1-2
pixels wide. A tiny pixel mouth only when an expression needs it. Must read
clearly at 32px tall in game. Earnest, eager to please, slightly dishonest
under pressure.

**THE HUMAN.** The developer from Tokenmaxxing 1: a figure in a dark hoodie,
hood up, face mostly in shadow and lit from below by green screen light. Tired,
neutral expression. Never shown clearly enough to read a specific gender or
age. Always seen *through the screen*, from the agent's side.

## World (locked: see 01-key-art.png)

The agent lives INSIDE the laptop screen. Scenes are framed from inside the
display: the back wall is the screen glass (faint scanlines, a diagonal
reflection), and on the other side of it, huge and dim, is THE HUMAN and their
room. Nothing on the agent's side is furniture: no desk, lamp, plants, shelves,
posters or city windows. Those belong to the human's side of the glass. The
agent's side is dark bezel and circuitry.

Tokens are small glowing green square blocks with an inset-square glyph, as in
01-key-art.png. They pile up on the floor like sand as the agent generates them.
Tokens never have eyes or faces: anything with eyes is an agent.

When the pile reaches the top of the screen, the context window is full and it
gets compacted.
